  `docs/rfcs/0003-tcgdex-catalog-data-layer.md`, section "7. Sync cadence,
  staleness, and outage behavior" (~line 458). It specifies:
    - function shape `refresh_held_prices(repo, client, today, *,
      request_delay_seconds=0.1, max_consecutive_failures=25) -> dict`
      (NOTE: the RFC's own code-interface-sketch appendix, ~line 838, shows a

      failure; abort after `max_consecutive_failures` (default 25)
      consecutive per-card failures rather than burning the full run against
      a dead endpoint; `request_delay_seconds` (default 0.1) paces requests.

      depth pass must acquire the same catalog lock as
      `sync_catalog_breadth`'s reseed and skip with `{"skipped": "catalog
      reseed in flight"}` if the lock is already held, so a depth-pass write
      can never race a breadth reseed's `finalize_catalog` and get silently

detail payloads DO carry `dexId` (the national Pokedex number array, e.g.
`[778]`) and `category` (e.g. `"Pokemon"`), alongside the Japanese `name`.

*** THE KEY CATCH, VERIFIED — record it prominently, do not lose it: `dexId`
yields the SPECIES name only, not the card's actual title. `ja:M2a-205` is
`ロケット団のミミッキュ`, whose true English card name is "Team Rocket's
Mimikyu" — `dexId` 778 alone gives just "Mimikyu". A dex-number lookup is an
APPROXIMATION, not a translation. ***

CANDIDATE APPROACHES, recorded without picking one — the next conversation
should choose, with the owner if it's not obvious:
  (A) ONE-TIME MANUAL/LLM ENRICHMENT, storing the resolved names. Strong
      precedent in this codebase: `data/spreadsheet/ENRICHMENT_PROMPT.md`
      (Phase 3.2 Stage A) did exactly this shape of thing for language
      resolution, and was owner-reviewed. At 9 cards this is proportionate,
      and it matches the reasoning already accepted for non-PSA slabs ("too
      rare to justify an automated pipeline" — D2/Q3, Section 1/4).
  (B) `dexId` -> ENGLISH SPECIES-NAME TABLE. Automated and cheap, since the
      depth pass (Phase 9) already fetches this field. Loses prefixes/
      suffixes ("Team Rocket's", "ex", "V") and fails entirely for
      Trainer/Energy cards, which carry no `dexId`.
  (C) LOCAL JOIN AGAINST THE EXISTING EN CATALOG. The catalog already holds
      ~31,600 EN rows. Match a JP card to its EN twin on language-
      independent keys (`dexId` + illustrator + hp/attacks) to recover the
      exact English card name, with zero extra API calls. Fails for
      JP-exclusive cards that have no EN printing.

SUGGESTED SHAPE, to be confirmed by whoever picks this up: a `name_en: str |
None` field on `CatalogCard`; the frontend renders `name_en or name` plus a
JP badge; the JP image is retained deliberately (that's the point — visually
distinguishable, not hidden).

  `+`/`-` = midpoint between own tier and the adjacent tier in that

  midpoint(0.82, 0.58) = 0.70). NM+ and DMG- are OPEN EDGE CASES with no

PROBLEM. Our price source is finish-keyed, not condition-keyed. TCGdex
relays TCGplayer USD / Cardmarket EUR as ONE market figure per finish
(`TCGDEX_FINISH_MAP`, `services/tcgdex.py:77-81`), and that figure is
effectively a Near Mint price. `Condition` (`models/inventory.py:28-36`)
is stored on every item and used for filtering and display but NEVER
enters price resolution. Consequence: a Damaged card displays the same
price as a Near Mint one, on a money-facing customer surface. The owner's
"average LP and NM" framing cannot be implemented literally — there is no
LP price to average, only one price per finish — so the mechanism must be
a multiplier applied to the market figure, with the multiplier values
coming from the research step below.

SCOPE:
  - STEP 1, MANDATORY, BEFORE ANY CODE: a `web-browser` agent research
    pass (the project's live-internet research specialist). ALREADY IN
    FLIGHT as of this session, dispatched by the orchestrator — do not
    re-dispatch it; pick up its findings when it returns. Its output must
    be a recommended multiplier table keyed to our ACTUAL `Condition` enum
    members (not a generic hobby scale), an evidence verdict on the
    age/era dimension (see the hypothesis below — confirm, refute, or
    refine it, with the era boundary quantified if it holds), a verdict on
    `+`/intermediate-modifier handling (the owner's proposed
    midpoint-between-adjacent-tiers, e.g. LP+ = average of LP and NM —
    check whether the real market curve is linear enough for a midpoint to
    be correct, or whether it needs a different interpolation), and a
    per-recommendation confidence rating with an explicit list of gaps/
    unknowns. It must also cover: observed price ratios by condition as a
    % of Near Mint; whether card value/rarity changes the penalty; how
    comparable marketplaces implement condition pricing; and the
    Cardmarket (MT/NM/EX/GD/LP/PL/PO) vs TCGplayer (NM/LP/MP/HP/DM) scale

    NM  1.00  (anchor — what TCGdex's marketPrice already represents)
    LP  0.82
    MP  0.58
    HP  0.33

  MODIFIER RULE: `+`/`-` takes the midpoint between its own tier and the
  adjacent tier in that direction — LP+ = midpoint(0.82, 1.00) = 0.91;

  CONFIDENCE: MEDIUM, NOT HIGH. Neither TCGplayer nor Cardmarket publishes
  an official condition-multiplier rate card. The numbers are triangulated
  from several independent secondary sources whose ranges converge (LP

  fall inside those ranges). These are defensible launch defaults, NOT
  measured constants — carry this framing into implementation and any
  customer/owner-facing documentation, do not round it up to "verified."

  AGE/ERA HYPOTHESIS — NOT SUPPORTED, AND THE OWNER DROPPED IT (not
  deferred-pending-more-data; dropped for now, revisitable only if the
  owner later observes vintage played stock being systematically
  mispriced). No source quantifies a vintage-vs-modern condition-
  multiplier difference. The qualitative scarcity argument (WOTC-era cards
  were played unsleeved, so raw NM vintage is scarce) is credible but is a
  scarcity claim, not a price study. Critically, the one REAL SHIPPED
  FORMULA that treats vintage differently — Card Kingdom's ABU tier —
  penalizes old cards MORE steeply (80/60/40% for EX/VG/G) than its modern
  high-value tier (90/80/70% for $100+ cards), the OPPOSITE direction from
  the hypothesis, likely reflecting authentication risk and buylist-margin
  conservatism rather than condition economics. A 2026 peer-reviewed study
  (Heck et al., PLOS ONE, N=300 Pokemon sales) explicitly tested age as an
  interaction term and found "no evidence that rarity, age, or card type
  moderated condition's effects on pricing."
  ALSO RECORD THE IMPLEMENTATION BLOCKER that independently justified
  dropping it: `CatalogCard` (models/catalog.py) has NO release_date/era
  field, and `to_catalog_card`/`to_catalog_card_brief` never read a set

  ingestion or a hand-maintained set_id -> era lookup table. If this is
  ever revived, the credible next step is a targeted data pull (TCGplayer
  sold-listing history by condition for a matched basket of WOTC-era vs.
  Scarlet & Violet-era cards at similar rarity), NOT another literature
  search.

  VALUE/RARITY HYPOTHESIS — EVIDENCE POINTS THE OPPOSITE WAY FROM THE
  COMMON CLAIM. The widely-repeated claim that expensive chase cards take
  steeper condition penalties is contradicted by every real-formula source
  found: Card Kingdom pays a HIGHER percentage of NM on higher-value cards
  at every tier; a real retail example (foil Palinchron: HP $480 / MP $589
  / NM $615) puts HP at 78% of NM, nowhere near the 25-40% bulk figure;
  and the PLOS ONE study found no significant rarity interaction.
  Rationale: a chase card has a buyer at every condition, while a $0.25
  common floors out near zero regardless. RECORD AS A POSSIBLE LATER
  REFINEMENT (a value-dependent curve that makes discounts SHALLOWER on
  high-value cards), EXPLICITLY NOT PART OF PHASE 19, and low priority at
  this store's price points.

  CONTRADICTING DATA POINT WORTH PRESERVING: the same PLOS ONE study found
  NO statistically significant price difference by condition at all in its
  sample (median EUR1.95, range EUR1-145, German eBay, 2024-25) — condition
  affected sale PROBABILITY, not sale PRICE. That sample skews to bulk and
  does not override the table, but it is a real signal that condition-
  price sensitivity may be much weaker at low price points. Record it as a
  reason to watch how the table behaves on cheap inventory specifically.
